Evidence and methods

The record behind the decision brief

This page shows what was tested, every comparison behind the recommendations, what each task looks like, and how the results were protected from wishful reading. The short version is the decision brief.

01 / Findings

What was tested, and what it showed

The work covered two kinds of input. Vision models and parsers read page images. Text models read extracted text to answer contract questions. Each track has its own data and scoring, so the scores are never pooled into one ranking.

Models

Read images: Qwen3-VL 8B and 32B, Qwen2.5-VL 7B and 32B, a 27B local build whose origin could not be verified (reported, not recommended), and three document parsers: Nemotron 3.5 Parse (0.63B), PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (0.9B) and olmOCR-2 (7B). DocLayout-YOLO detects page regions.

Read text only: Qwen3 32B, gpt-oss-20b and Nemotron 3 Nano 30B (through a hosted API). They were tested on contract questions, not on images. Nemotron 3.5 Parse and Nemotron 3 Nano are different models.

Not tested

Handwriting, phone photos of documents, very poor scans, native spreadsheets (read those directly from the file), tables that run across pages, whole multi-document packets, and any private documents. These limit where the recommendations apply until a pilot measures them.

Why the highest score in a comparison is not always the recommendation

Each comparison ranks one measure. A setup can lead on word overlap and still lose numbers. On the final page test, for example, the PaddleOCR pipeline led word overlap (0.922) but recovered 77.1% of numbers, while Nemotron Parse recovered 88.2% and was faster. For documents with amounts, the recommendation favours numbers. Scores from different sets, such as the round-2 masking check and the round-3 final test, are never compared directly, because the pages and setups differ.

Weights are not the whole memory budget. Context, image processing, runtime overhead and simultaneous requests all need room, and fit by weight does not guarantee two models can run together. Speed depends mostly on memory bandwidth: about 1.8 TB/s on the RTX 5090, 0.86 TB/s on an L40S and 0.3 TB/s on an L4. The target cards will therefore be slower than the desktop, and large models will slow down most. That is an estimate from specifications, not a measurement.

What each task looks like

What the models were shown, and what came back

These are illustrations written for this page. They are not benchmark items and not recorded model outputs. They show the shape of each task, so the scores have something concrete behind them.

Illustration · tables

A table image becomes rows and columns

Input: an image of a table, cropped from a page.

Budget categoryYear 1Year 2
Personnel$120,000$124,800
Equipment$35,000$0
Travel$8,500$9,000

Expected output: the same grid as structured rows, with each amount in its own cell. Table similarity compares the structure and cell text with the reference. Numbers recovered checks that the reference amounts appear. A reader that moves $124,800 into the Year 1 column keeps the text but breaks the meaning.

Illustration · whole pages

A page image becomes text in reading order

Input: an image of a full page with a heading, paragraphs, a small table and a figure.

Expected: "Section 4. Budget justification. The total award requested is $1,250,000 over two years..."
Weak reading: "Section 4. Budget justification. The total award requested is $125,000 over two years..."

The weak reading changes one number. Word overlap barely moves, but the amount is wrong. That is why every page result reports numbers recovered beside word overlap.

Illustration · charts

A question about a chart, or the chart's data

Chart question: "How much higher was Q2 than Q1?" Expected answer: 15. Numbers within 5% count as correct.
Chart to table: rebuild the data: Q1 40, Q2 55, Q3 48. The score is the share of values recovered.

Illustration · cropped text

A cropped paragraph becomes exact text

Input: an image of one paragraph cut from a scan, not a whole page. Output: the paragraph's text. Character error counts how many characters would need to change to match the reference. 1.2% means about one character in eighty.

Illustration · contract questions

A question, an answer and its evidence

Passage [block B12]: "Either party may terminate this Agreement upon thirty (30) days' written notice."
CUAD-style question: Is there a termination-for-convenience clause?  →  Yes. Evidence: B12.
ContractNLI-style statement: "The agreement can be ended without cause."  →  Supported. Evidence: B12.

The model reads extracted text, not an image. It returns an answer and points to the evidence block, and code copies the exact source text so a reviewer can check it.

What a number failure looks like

Clean text is not the same as correct amounts

Two readings of the same budget line: "Equipment $35,000" and "Equipment $3,500". Both are fluent, and only one is right. Word-based scores treat them as nearly identical. Recovered-number scores catch the difference, and a field-level check, the next step for any pilot, would catch whether the amount sits in the right field.

Contract track

Contract questions: what was tested, and who did best

The purpose. To see whether locally run language models can find the right information in a contract and say whether a document supports a statement, while pointing to evidence a reviewer can check. This is a test of extraction and reading comprehension. It is not legal advice, and it does not test human-level legal judgement.

The data. CUAD (Contract Understanding Atticus Dataset) holds commercial contracts annotated with clause types. The questions ask whether a clause is present, and some answers are "absent". ContractNLI holds confidentiality agreements paired with statements. The model must decide whether the document supports, contradicts or does not mention each statement. Both are public. Neither represents proposals or awards, so transfer to those documents needs its own test.

How to read the counts. Each task has 30 questions on 30 documents. A question counts as correct only if the answer and its evidence pass the frozen checks. Failed or cut-off answers count as wrong. "Cut off" means the model reached its 16,000-token output limit before finishing. Thinking or reasoning text uses part of that limit, which is why the high-reasoning setting was cut off 19 times in 60.

Settings, not separate models. gpt-oss-20b low and high are two reasoning settings of one model. Qwen3 32B thinking off and on are the same. The hosted Nemotron row is one configuration of that model through an API.

Conclusion

Recommended for clause and statement checks: Qwen3 32B with thinking off, the best ContractNLI result (18 of 30), with no answers cut off. It needs a 48 GB card to run beside a parser. Smaller alternative: gpt-oss-20b at low reasoning (14 of 30), which fits a 24 GB card. What did not meet the need: high-reasoning settings under this output limit, and every configuration on CUAD, where none beat answering "absent". Next: better retrieval of the right passage, clause-level evidence blocks and a larger output budget are untested ideas for the pilot. Every answer needs a reviewer.

04 / Methods

How the results were protected

A recommendation is only as good as the test behind it. These safeguards are what make the recommendations defensible, and each one exists to stop a specific mistake.

Defined jobs and answer keys

Each task measures the capability the work needs, scored by reference answers and code, so no model's opinion decides a score.

Frozen examples, prompts and scorers

Nothing could be moved after the results were seen. Every run was checked against a fingerprint of the cases, requests and scorers before it counted.

Explore first, then confirm on unseen data

Methods were chosen on development examples, then checked once on separate examples. Where the same examples were reused, the result is labelled exploratory.

Pass rules written down in advance

For the final tests, what counted as a win, including a limit on losing numbers, was set before scoring. A better text score could not make up for lost numbers.

Paired comparisons with uncertainty

Models were compared on the same examples, with 95% intervals. The final tests resample by source document, so related examples are not counted as independent. The round-2 chart intervals treat each question as independent, which makes them look somewhat more precise than they are.

Failures stay in

Failed and cut-off answers count as zero and were never rerun to rescue a score. Two test arms that failed because of software errors, before any model was called, were logged, excluded and rerun under a recorded amendment.

The chart questions: where they came from

The 1,322 confirmation questions come from the validation split of ChartQA, a public dataset of real charts from statistics and news sources. Its questions are partly written by people and partly generated by machine. Round 1 had used ChartQA's test split, so round 2 drew on the validation split, which no model in this project had seen. Charts were divided by a fixed hash, one third for development and two thirds for confirmation, so no chart appears in both. That gives 1,322 questions about 722 charts. Several questions share a chart, so they are not 1,322 independent documents. The questions test reading values, comparing them and simple arithmetic, the same skills a reader needs for charts in reports and award attachments. They are a component test, not proof that a whole packet will be read correctly. "Unseen in this project" also does not mean the charts were absent from the models' training data.

Three-answer voting, and what "percentage points" means

Three-answer voting (vote3) takes three stored answers to the same question, the baseline, self-check and upscaled-image answers, and keeps the majority. Answers within 5% count as the same, and a three-way tie goes to the self-check answer. It needs no extra model calls once the three runs exist, but it does need all three runs. A percentage point is a plain difference between two percentages: moving from 70% to 74.5% is a gain of 4.5 points.

The 7B gained more, but it still finished well below the 32B (60.3% against 84.6%). A bigger improvement does not mean a better final score. "No reliable gain" for the 8B means the change was too small to tell from zero, not that voting can never help an 8B model.

Measures, in plain language

Table similarity (TEDS)
Tree-Edit-Distance-based Similarity. It compares the recovered table's structure and cell text with the reference, from 0 to 1. 0.852 is a similarity, not "85.2% of tables were fully correct".
Word overlap (word F1)
A balance between recovering the reference words and not adding extra ones, from 0 to 1. It is not overall accuracy and does not check numbers.
Numbers recovered (numeric recall)
The share of reference numbers that appear in the output. It does not prove each number sits in the right field or row.
Character error
The share of characters that would need to change to match the reference. Lower is better.
Chart accuracy
The share of chart questions answered to match the reference, with 5% tolerance on numbers.
Strict correctness
For contract questions, the answer and its evidence must both pass the frozen checks.
Interval
The range the true difference plausibly lies in, given the sample. If it includes zero, the difference is not established.
Development, held-out, pre-registered
Development examples are for exploring. Held-out examples are kept aside and scored once. Pre-registered means the pass rule was written down before scoring.
Parser, pipeline
A document parser is a small model trained to turn a page or table image into text or structure. A parser's official pipeline adds its own layout step, which splits the page first and reads each part, before reading.
Processing failure, cut off
An attempt that returned no usable output, or stopped at the output limit. Both score zero.

What transfers, and what must be retested

The ranking of models on these public tasks should carry over to similar documents, and the method carries over completely. Absolute accuracy on your documents, speed on your hardware, and how two models behave together on one card do not carry over. Measure them in a pilot. Two of thirteen same-model comparisons between the laptop and the desktop did not match in round 1. Rerunning them on the desktop reproduced its earlier scores exactly (260 of 260 cases), so the difference is machine-specific and its cause is still open.

What this evaluation cannot approve

It supports choosing models and hardware for a pilot. It cannot approve unattended extraction (no model reached 99% on numbers), claim accuracy on private documents, promise speed or cost on an L4 or L40S, or establish security. The final advisory lists every open question.

05 / Next steps

Building the routed service

The proposed design, described in the decision brief, sends each part of a document to the tool that did best on that kind of reading. It must also meet these requirements, none of which is built or measured yet.

  • Keep the original file. Add each derived result as a new version and never overwrite the source.
  • Keep document, page and region identity, so results can be recombined and checked without losing context or counting text twice.
  • Use native text and spreadsheet data where it exists, instead of sending everything through a vision model.
  • Record the model and settings, processing state, unread regions, failures and reviewer decisions for every item.
  • Make every stage resumable, with checkpoints and bounded retries, so an interruption does not restart everything.
  • Measure the assembled service end to end. Strong component scores do not prove the parts work together.

The implementation roadmap sets out the order of work.
