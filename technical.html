Models

Read images: Qwen3-VL 8B and 32B, Qwen2.5-VL 7B and 32B, a 27B local build whose origin could not be verified (reported, not recommended), and three document parsers: Nemotron 3.5 Parse (0.63B), PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (0.9B) and olmOCR-2 (7B). DocLayout-YOLO detects page regions.

Read text only: Qwen3 32B, gpt-oss-20b and Nemotron 3 Nano 30B through a hosted API, tested on contract questions, not images. Nemotron 3.5 Parse and Nemotron 3 Nano are different models.

Not tested

Handwriting, phone photos of documents, very poor scans, native spreadsheets (read those directly from the file), tables that run across pages, whole multi-document packets, and any private documents. A pilot has to measure these before the recommendations apply to them.

!

Why the highest score is not always the recommendation.

Each comparison ranks one measure. On the final page test the PaddleOCR pipeline led word overlap (0.922) but recovered 77.1% of numbers; Nemotron Parse recovered 88.2% and was faster. Scores from different sets, such as the round-2 masking check and the round-3 final test, are never compared directly.

What the models were shown,
and what came back.

These are illustrations written for this page, not benchmark items and not recorded model outputs. They show the shape of each task so the scores have something concrete behind them.

Illustration · tables

A table image becomes rows and columns

Input: an image of a table cropped from a page.

Expected: the same grid, each amount in its own cell. Table similarity compares structure and cell text with the reference; numbers recovered checks the amounts appear. Moving $124,800 into Year 1 keeps the text but breaks the meaning.

Illustration · whole pages

A page image becomes text in reading order

Input: a full page with a heading, paragraphs, a small table and a figure.

Expected: "...The total award requested is $1,250,000 over two years..."
Weak:     "...The total award requested is $125,000 over two years..."

One number changes. Word overlap barely moves, but the amount is wrong. That is why page results always report numbers recovered beside word overlap.

Illustration · charts

A question about a chart, or its data

Chart question: "How much higher was Q2 than Q1?" Expected: 15, with 5% tolerance on numbers. Chart to table: rebuild Q1 40, Q2 55, Q3 48; the score is the share of values recovered.

Illustration · cropped text

A cropped paragraph becomes exact text

Input: one paragraph cut from a scan, not a whole page. Output: its text. Character error counts the characters that would need to change to match the reference; 1.2% is about one in eighty.

Illustration · contracts

A question, an answer and its evidence

[B12] "Either party may terminate this Agreement upon thirty (30) days' written notice."
CUAD-style: termination for convenience?  →  Yes · evidence B12
ContractNLI-style: "Can be ended without cause"  →  Supported · B12

The model reads extracted text, returns an answer and points to the evidence block; code copies the exact source text so a reviewer can check it.

What a number failure looks like

Clean text is not correct amounts

"Equipment $35,000" and "Equipment $3,500" are both fluent; only one is right. Word scores treat them as nearly identical. Recovered-number scores catch the difference, and a field-level check, the next step for any pilot, catches whether it sits in the right field.

CONTRACT TRACK

Contract questions:
what was tested, who did best.

The purpose. To see whether locally run language models can find the right information in a contract and say whether a document supports a statement, while pointing to evidence a reviewer can check. It tests extraction and reading comprehension, not legal advice or human-level legal judgement.

The data. CUAD (Contract Understanding Atticus Dataset) holds commercial contracts annotated with clause types; its questions ask whether a clause is present, and some answers are "absent". ContractNLI pairs confidentiality agreements with statements the model must judge as supported, contradicted or not mentioned. Neither represents proposals or awards, so transfer to those needs its own test.

How to read the counts. Each task has 30 questions on 30 documents. A question counts as correct only if the answer and its evidence pass the frozen checks; failed or cut-off answers count as wrong. "Cut off" means the model hit its 16,000-token output limit before finishing. Reasoning text uses part of that limit, which is why the high-reasoning setting was cut off 19 times in 60.

Settings, not separate models. gpt-oss-20b low and high are two reasoning settings of one model; Qwen3 32B thinking off and on are the same. The hosted Nemotron row is one configuration of that model through an API.

04 / METHODS

How the results
were protected.

A recommendation is only as good as the test behind it. Each safeguard here exists to stop a specific mistake: moving the goalposts, trusting a lucky sample, hiding failures, or letting a cleaner-looking answer stand in for correct numbers.

Open a panel for the detail, including where the chart questions came from, how three-answer voting works, and what every measure means.

The safeguards

Answer keys, not opinions. Every score comes from reference answers and code; no model graded another.
Frozen tests. Examples, prompts and scorers were fixed before results were seen, and every run was checked against a fingerprint of them before it counted.
Explore, then confirm. Methods were chosen on development examples, then checked once on separate examples; reused examples are labelled exploratory.
Pass rules in advance. For the final tests, what counted as a win, including a limit on losing numbers, was written down before scoring.
Paired comparisons with uncertainty. Models were compared on the same examples with 95% intervals. The final tests resample by source document, so related examples are not treated as independent. The round-2 chart intervals treat each question as independent, which makes them look somewhat more precise than they are.
Failures stay in. Failed and cut-off answers count as zero and were never rerun to rescue a score. Two test arms that failed because of software errors, before any model was called, were logged, excluded and rerun under a recorded amendment.

Where the 1,322 chart questions came from

From the validation split of ChartQA, a public dataset of real charts from statistics and news sources, with questions partly written by people and partly generated by machine. Round 1 had used ChartQA's test split, so round 2 drew on validation data no model in this project had seen. Charts were divided by a fixed hash, one third for development and two thirds for confirmation, so no chart appears in both: 1,322 questions about 722 charts. Several questions share a chart, so they are not 1,322 independent documents. The questions test reading values, comparing them and simple arithmetic, the skills charts in reports and award attachments need. They are a component test, not proof a whole packet will be read correctly, and "unseen in this project" does not mean absent from the models' training data.

Three-answer voting, and what "percentage points" means

Voting (vote3) takes three stored answers to the same question, the baseline, self-check and upscaled-image answers, and keeps the majority; answers within 5% count as the same, and a three-way tie goes to the self-check answer. It needs no extra calls once the three runs exist, but it needs all three runs. A percentage point is a plain difference between two percentages: 70% to 74.5% is a gain of 4.5 points.

ModelBaselineWith vote3Change
Qwen2.5-VL 7B (laptop)55.8%60.3%+4.5 points (interval +3.0 to +6.1)

The 7B gained more but still finished well below the 32B (60.3% against 84.6%): a bigger improvement is not a better final score. "No reliable gain" for the 8B means the change was too small to tell from zero, not that voting can never help an 8B model.

Measures in plain language
Table similarity (TEDS)
Tree-Edit-Distance-based Similarity: how closely the recovered table's structure and cell text match the reference, 0 to 1. 0.852 is a similarity, not "85.2% of tables fully correct".
Word overlap (word F1)
A balance between recovering the reference words and not adding extra ones, 0 to 1. Not overall accuracy; does not check numbers.
Numbers recovered (numeric recall)
Share of reference numbers that appear in the output. Does not prove each is in the right field or row.
Character error
Share of characters that would need to change to match the reference. Lower is better.
Chart accuracy
Share of chart questions answered to match the reference, with 5% tolerance on numbers.
Strict correctness
For contract questions, the answer and its evidence must both pass the frozen checks.
Interval
The range the true difference plausibly lies in, given the sample. If it includes zero, the difference is not established.
Development, held-out, pre-registered
Development examples are for exploring; held-out examples are kept aside and scored once; pre-registered means the pass rule was written down first.
Parser and pipeline
A document parser is a small model trained to turn a page or table image into text or structure. Its official pipeline adds a layout step that splits the page and reads each part.
Processing failure, cut off
An attempt that returned no usable output, or stopped at the output limit. Both score zero.
What transfers to your setup, and what must be retested

The ranking of models on these public tasks should carry over to similar documents, and the method carries over completely. Absolute accuracy on your documents, speed on your hardware and how two models behave together on one card do not; measure them in a pilot. Two of thirteen same-model comparisons between the laptop and the desktop did not match in round 1; rerunning them on the desktop reproduced its earlier scores exactly (260 of 260 cases), so the difference is machine-specific and its cause is still open.

What this evaluation cannot approve

It supports choosing models and hardware for a pilot. It cannot approve unattended extraction (no model reached 99% on numbers), claim accuracy on private documents, promise speed or cost on an L4 or L40S, or establish security. The final advisory lists every open question.

Building the
routed service.

The proposed service sends each part of a document to the tool that did best on that kind of reading. None of it is built or measured yet; these are its requirements.

01 / PRESERVE

Keep the source

Never overwrite the original file. Add each derived result as a new version, with document, page and region identity so results can be recombined and checked.

02 / REUSE

Read native data

Use native text and spreadsheet data where it exists instead of sending everything through a vision model.

03 / RECORD

Log every decision

Record model and settings, processing state, unread regions, failures and reviewer decisions for every item. Make every stage resumable with bounded retries.

04 / MEASURE

Test it end to end

Strong component scores do not prove the parts work together. Measure the assembled service on representative documents.

The decision brief describes the six-step service, batching for a backlog and a bounded pilot. The roadmap sets out the order of work.
