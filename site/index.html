TAYLER ERBE / DECISION BRIEF · OPEN-WEIGHT DOCUMENT AI

Which model should read your documents, and what will it cost to run?

Research proposals, procurement packets, contracts and reports keep their most useful facts in tables, charts, scans and clauses. I tested eleven open-weight models on public stand-ins for that work, confirmed the leaders on data they had never seen, and priced the hardware to run them privately.

Short answer: use small specialised models, not one big one. The recommended document readers fit on one 24 GB data-centre GPU. Keep a reviewer on amounts and contract answers.

L4 OR L40S?

The answer depends on contracts.

The L4 covers the document-reading routes: tables, pages, charts, text regions and layout. The L40S mainly buys the 32B contract model and headroom. On the public contract questions the 32B led the 20B on ContractNLI (18 against 14 of 30) and they tied on CUAD. Whether that gap is worth about $10,000 more depends on your contract fields and the volume you need to process. A short pilot can measure both before you buy.

Speeds were measured on the 32 GB desktop. They compare models fairly but are not speeds for an L4 or L40S. Purchase price leaves out engineering, review time, maintenance and energy. Running models on your own hardware keeps documents in your environment, but it does not make the system secure by itself. Hardware and budget guide ↗

04 / METHOD

How the decision was made,
and how I would make it for you.

The method matters more than any single score. It is the same six steps whether the documents are research awards, procurement packets or contracts.

  1. Define the jobs

    Split the documents into the kinds of reading they need, and name the failure that matters for each. Here, that was losing numbers.

  2. Freeze fair tests

    Pick public stand-ins with answer keys, fix the questions and the scoring code, and let no model grade another.

  3. Shortlist by budget

    Choose candidates that fit the hardware you can afford, from small parsers to 32B models.

  4. Explore, then confirm

    Explore on development data, write down what will count as a win, then test once on data no model has seen.

  5. Price the fit

    Map the winning setup to a hardware tier and a dated budget, separating measured speed from estimates.

  6. Validate on real documents

    Label a representative set of your own documents and measure field accuracy before approving anything.

Why it took several rounds

Each round answered a question the previous one raised. None simply reran tests until a good number appeared. Pick a round.

Why it stopped: the leads were either confirmed or bounded. More public benchmarks would add little, so the next evidence has to come from real documents. Full methods and definitions ↗

From evaluation
to a working service.

A mixed PDF is not one task. The proposed service sends each part of a document to the tool that did best on that kind of reading, keeps every answer linked to its source page, and leaves the decision with a reviewer.

  1. ReceiveKeep the original file untouched.
  2. IdentifyFind pages, tables, charts and text. Use native text where it exists.
  3. RouteSend each part to its recommended model.
  4. CombineAssemble one record, linked to source pages.
  5. ReviewShow values beside the page. Flag numbers and unread parts.
  6. ExportSend approved data to search, reports or other systems.

Build a review set

About 100 representative documents, including difficult scans, with two reviewers on a subset.

03 / MEASURE

Run the routes

Field accuracy, numbers, traceability, processing time and correction effort on the target server.

04 / DECIDE

Scale or adapt

Scale, adjust retrieval or prompts, or fine-tune a model such as Qwen3-VL 8B, based on the measured errors.

Clearing a backlog vs keeping up. A first pass over an archive can run in batches: load one model, process every waiting table, then switch, so one GPU never holds everything at once. After that, new documents arrive in smaller volumes. How long a backlog takes cannot be estimated from these tests; the pilot measures it.
