Decision brief · Open-weight document AI on your own hardware

Which open-weight model should read your documents, and what will it cost to run?

Research proposals, procurement packets, contracts and reports keep their most useful facts in tables, charts, scans and clauses. I tested eleven open-weight models on public stand-ins for that work, confirmed the leaders on data they had never seen, and priced the hardware to run them privately.

The short answer

Use small specialised models, not one big one. A 0.9-billion-parameter table reader beat a 32-billion-parameter vision model on financial tables. The recommended document-reading models all fit on one 24 GB data-centre GPU, an L4-class server planned at about $6,000 to $10,000. Keep a person reviewing amounts and contract answers: no model recovered 99% of the numbers.

Why the top score is not always the recommendation

In some comparisons another setup has a higher text score. The PaddleOCR official pipeline, for example, had the best word overlap on pages (0.922), but it recovered only 77.1% of the numbers. These recommendations favour keeping numbers, because budgets, awards and invoices fail on numbers first.

L4 or L40S?

The L4 covers the document-reading routes: tables, pages, charts, text regions and layout. The L40S mainly buys the 32B contract model and headroom. On the public contract questions the 32B led the 20B on ContractNLI (18 against 14 of 30) and they tied on CUAD. Whether that gap is worth about $10,000 more depends on your contract fields and the volume you need to process. A short pilot can measure both before you buy.

Speeds were measured on the 32 GB desktop. They compare models fairly but are not speeds for an L4 or L40S. Purchase price leaves out engineering, review time, maintenance and energy. Running models on your own hardware keeps documents in your environment, but it does not make the system secure by itself. Details: hardware and budget guide.

04 / Method

How the decision was made, and how I would make it for you

The method matters more than any single score. It is the same six steps whether the documents are research awards, procurement packets or contracts.

  1. Define the jobsSplit the documents into the kinds of reading they need, and name the failure that matters for each. Here, that was losing numbers.
  2. Freeze fair testsPick public stand-ins with answer keys, fix the questions and the scoring code, and let no model grade another.
  3. Shortlist by budgetChoose candidates that fit the hardware you can afford, from small parsers to 32B models.
  4. Explore, then confirmExplore on development data, write down what will count as a win, then test once on data no model has seen.
  5. Price the fitMap the winning setup to a hardware tier and a dated budget, separating measured speed from estimates.
  6. Validate on real documentsLabel a representative set of your own documents and measure field accuracy before approving anything.

Why it took several rounds

Each round answered a question the previous one raised. None of them simply reran tests until a good number appeared.

Round 1Vision models
QuestionWhich local vision model reads each part of a document, on a 12 GB laptop and a 32 GB desktop?
TestFive vision models and several prompting methods on frozen public sets for text, tables, charts and pages.
FindingThe 8B model matched the 32B on text and tables. Prompting methods helped some models and hurt others.
DecisionRoute by model and task. Test the promising methods properly.
Round 2Pre-registered
QuestionDo the chosen methods hold on data no model has seen?
TestThree-answer voting on 1,322 new chart questions and layout masking on 80 new pages, with the pass rules written first.
FindingVoting helped the 7B and 32B, not the 8B. Masking cleaned up text but lost numbers.
DecisionKeep voting optional, keep masking experimental, and look for specialist tools.
Contract trackText models
QuestionCan models answer contract questions with evidence a reviewer can check?
TestCUAD and ContractNLI public contracts, quoting versus selecting evidence by ID, and a controlled reassessment of a hosted model.
FindingSelecting evidence by ID removed copy failures. The hosted model's poor first score was a configuration problem.
DecisionCompare every candidate on the same 60 documents.
Round 3New candidates
QuestionDo specialist document parsers or gpt-oss-20b beat the incumbents?
TestThree parsers on every earlier page and table set, and gpt-oss-20b on the contract sets.
FindingLeads emerged: PaddleOCR-VL for tables, Nemotron Parse for pages. They were found by looking, so they were not yet proof.
DecisionWrite the two hypotheses down and test them on fresh data.
Round 3b and 3cConfirmation
QuestionDo the leads hold on unseen data, with the same output limit for every model?
Test100 new financial tables and 80 new pages, the parsers' official pipelines, budget checks, and Qwen3 32B on the 60 contract documents. 3c reran two arms that had failed for software reasons.
FindingThe table lead was confirmed. The page lead held on text but was inconclusive on numbers. Official pipelines did not beat a single pass.
DecisionClose the evaluation, route by task, and validate on real documents.

Why it stopped: the leads were either confirmed or bounded. More public benchmarks would add little, so the next evidence has to come from real documents. Full methods and definitions →

05 / Next steps

From evaluation to a working service

A mixed PDF is not one task. The proposed service sends each part of a document to the tool that did best on that kind of reading, keeps every answer linked to its source page, and leaves the decision with a reviewer.

  1. ReceiveKeep the original file untouched.
  2. IdentifyFind pages, tables, charts and text, and use native text where it exists.
  3. RouteSend each part to its recommended model.
  4. CombineAssemble the results into one record, linked to source pages.
  5. ReviewShow proposed values beside the page. Flag numbers and unread parts.
  6. ExportSend approved data to search, reports or other systems.

Clearing a backlog vs keeping up

A first pass over an existing archive can run in batches: load one model, process every waiting table, then switch, so a single GPU is never asked to hold everything at once. After that, new and changed documents arrive in smaller volumes. How long the backlog takes cannot be estimated from these tests. It depends on pages per file, how often each route is needed, the target server and review effort, and the pilot should measure it.

A bounded pilot

  1. Agree the fields that matter and where the documents come from.
  2. Label about 100 representative documents, including difficult scans, with two reviewers on a subset.
  3. Run the recommended routes and measure field accuracy, numbers, traceability, time and correction effort.
  4. Decide whether to scale, adapt or fine-tune, using the measured errors.

Fine-tuning a model such as Qwen3-VL 8B on your own labelled documents may help. It was not tested here, and it cannot fix text that was never read in the first place.
